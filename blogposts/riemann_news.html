Yesterday, Levent Alpöge, a mathematician who works for Anthropic and is known for past landmark AI-powered math results such as the disproof of the Jacobian conjecture, announced that he had a proof of the (Generalized!) Riemann Hypothesis. He clarified that this was a joint effort between himself and an internal Claude model—rather than simply asking the model for a proof, he guided it over several weeks using his mathematical expertise (Alpöge holds a PhD in math).

The proof is only available as millions of lines of Lean; Alpöge claimed that the natural language proof exceeded 250,000 pages and did not bother publishing it. As of Saturday evening, the proof has been independently verified in Lean.

The proof has now been verified independently by the Lean community, using con-leche, a piece of Lean that itself is formally verified all the way down to the axioms of set theory. This is a much higher degree of verification than a typical human paper accepted to a math journal. The proof is correct.

— Scott Aaronson

It is too early to tell the methodologies used in this gargantuan proof. For now, we only know that the conjecture is true.

Impacts

The Riemann Hypothesis was the crown jewel open problem of modern mathematics. Indeed, the consensus among experts in the field was that we were decades away from its resolution, even with AI assistance. It was expected that a whole new branch of math would need to be invented wholesale. Obviously, it is a Millenium Prize Problem.

One of the most interesting implications of the hypothesis is on the distribution of prime numbers. If $\pi(x)$ is the number of primes less than or equal to $x$, it was previously known that, for some positive $c$, $$\pi(x)=\text{li}(x)+O(x\exp\Big[-c\frac{(\log x)^{3/5}}{(\log\log x)^{1/5}}\Big]),$$ where li is the logarithmic integral.

Because RH is true, we know $$\pi(x)=\text{li}(x)+O(\sqrt x\log x).$$
